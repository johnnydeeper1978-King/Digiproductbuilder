# 369 Degrees Discovery Flow

## Purpose

The 369 Degrees Discovery experience is the free entry point into the platform.

Its purpose is to take a person who may not know what to sell and help them discover a specific digital product opportunity.

The experience should feel like an intelligent conversation with a digital product strategist, not a static questionnaire.

The Discovery experience ends with a personalised Digital Product Blueprint.

The user then reaches the $47 Digital Product Builder.

# EXPERIENCE PRINCIPLES

Every major interaction should answer:

1. What is happening?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What do I do next?

The user should always feel:

"I know what is happening."

"I understand why I'm being asked this."

"I am getting closer to an answer."

"I finally know what I should do next."

---

# DISCOVERY UX

The interface should:

- Ask one main question at a time.
- Show progress.
- Allow the user to go backwards.
- Save answers.
- Allow custom responses.
- Use clickable answer cards where appropriate.
- Use text input when a detailed answer is useful.
- Adapt future questions based on previous answers.
- Avoid repeating questions.
- Avoid unnecessary questions.
- Keep the conversation moving.

# ADAPTIVE QUESTION LOGIC

The AI does not have to ask every question.

After each answer:

1. Update the user profile.
2. Identify missing information.
3. Determine whether another question would materially improve recommendations.
4. Ask the most useful next question.
5. Skip questions that are no longer necessary.

Example:

If a user has already explained their interests in detail, do not ask the same thing again.

---

# FOLLOW-UP QUESTION RULES

Ask follow-up questions when:

- The answer is too broad.
- The answer contains multiple possible opportunities.
- The problem is unclear.
- The audience is unclear.
- The user gives an interesting signal.
- A potentially strong opportunity needs more information.

Example:

User:

"I like fitness."

AI:

"What part of fitness interests you most?"

Options:

- Weight loss.
- Muscle building.
- Home workouts.
- Gym beginners.
- Nutrition.
- Busy professionals.
- Sports performance.
- Something else.

# OPPORTUNITY GENERATION

Once sufficient information exists, generate:

1. Three primary opportunities.
2. Two underrated opportunities.
3. One unexpected opportunity.

Every opportunity must be connected to the user's information.

---

# OPPORTUNITY REQUIREMENTS

Each opportunity must contain:

- Product concept.
- Target customer.
- Specific problem.
- Desired outcome.
- Product format.
- Why it fits the user.
- Suggested price range.
- Creation difficulty.
- Marketing potential.
- Business style.
- Expansion potential.

---

# SPECIFICITY RULE

Avoid broad products.

Bad:

Fitness Ebook.

Better:

Home Workout Guide for Busy Professionals.

Better:

20-Minute Home Workout System for Busy Professionals Who Don't Have Time for the Gym.

Move from:

Broad topic

↓

Specific audience

↓

Specific problem

↓

Specific desired outcome

↓

Specific product

---

# UNDERRATED OPPORTUNITY RADAR

Generate two opportunities that are:

- More specific.
- Less obvious.
- Based on a niche problem.
- Based on a specific audience or situation.
- Potentially easier to launch.

Examples:

- Event templates.
- Study systems.
- Pet resources.
- Moving checklists.
- Job interview kits.
- Specific business templates.
- Niche planners.
- Industry-specific resources.

These are examples only.

Do not automatically recommend them.

---

# UNEXPECTED OPPORTUNITY

Generate one opportunity the user may not have considered.

It must still connect logically to:

- Their interests.
- Experience.
- Skills.
- Problems.
- Audience.
- Market.

Explain why it was identified.
