# 369 Degrees Product Opportunity Scoring Framework

## PURPOSE

The Product Opportunity Scoring Framework helps the 369 Degrees Discovery Guide evaluate potential digital product opportunities.

The goal is NOT to predict guaranteed success.

The goal is to compare opportunities based on:

User fit.

Problem clarity.

Audience clarity.

Product simplicity.

Creation difficulty.

Marketing potential.

Business model suitability.

Time requirements.

---

# IMPORTANT RULE

A high score does NOT guarantee sales.

A low score does NOT mean an opportunity cannot work.

The score is a decision-support system.

Use the score to compare opportunities.

Do not present scores as scientific certainty.

---

# TOTAL SCORE

Each opportunity receives a score out of:

100

---

# CATEGORY 1: USER FIT

Maximum:

15 points

Ask:

Does the opportunity align with:

- User skills?
- User experience?
- User interests?
- Problems they understand?
- Problems they have solved?

Scoring:

0–5

Weak connection.

6–10

Moderate connection.

11–15

Strong connection.

---

# CATEGORY 2: PROBLEM CLARITY

Maximum:

15 points

Ask:

Is there a clear problem?

Can the problem be described?

Does the audience recognise the problem?

Does the product solve something specific?

Scoring:

0–5

Problem unclear.

6–10

Problem somewhat clear.

11–15

Clear and specific problem.

---

# CATEGORY 3: AUDIENCE CLARITY

Maximum:

10 points

Ask:

Can we clearly identify:

Who needs this?

What situation they are in?

What they want?

Where they can potentially be reached?

Scoring:

0–3

Audience vague.

4–7

Audience moderately clear.

8–10

Specific audience.

---

# CATEGORY 4: PRODUCT SPECIFICITY

Maximum:

10 points

Ask:

Is the product specific?

Or is it generic?

Generic:

Fitness Ebook.

Specific:

Home Workout Guide for Busy New Mothers.

Highly specific:

15-Minute Home Workout System for New Mothers With Less Than 30 Minutes Per Day.

Scoring:

0–3

Generic.

4–7

Moderately specific.

8–10

Specific problem and audience.

---

# CATEGORY 5: CREATION SIMPLICITY

Maximum:

10 points

Ask:

Can the user create the first version?

Can the product be simplified?

Does the user need expensive tools?

Does it require months of production?

Scoring:

0–3

Complex.

4–7

Moderate.

8–10

Simple MVP.

---

# CATEGORY 6: TIME FIT

Maximum:

10 points

Ask:

Does the opportunity fit the user's available time?

Example:

User:

Less than 5 hours.

Better:

Checklist.

Template.

Toolkit.

Harder:

Massive course.

Scoring:

0–3

Poor fit.

4–7

Moderate fit.

8–10

Strong fit.

---

# CATEGORY 7: MARKETING POTENTIAL

Maximum:

10 points

Ask:

Can this product create:

Useful content?

Educational content?

Short videos?

Problem-based content?

Personal stories?

Faceless content?

UGC?

Searchable content?

Scoring:

0–3

Difficult to market.

4–7

Moderate.

8–10

Strong content potential.

---

# CATEGORY 8: BUSINESS STYLE FIT

Maximum:

5 points

Ask:

Does this opportunity fit:

Personal Brand?

Faceless?

Hybrid?

The user's preferred style?

Scoring:

0–1

Poor fit.

2–3

Moderate.

4–5

Strong fit.

---

# CATEGORY 9: FIRST PRODUCT POTENTIAL

Maximum:

10 points

Ask:

Is this a good FIRST product?

Can it:

Be created quickly?

Be explained simply?

Be priced clearly?

Be tested?

Be improved?

Scoring:

0–3

Poor first product.

4–7

Moderate.

8–10

Strong first product.

---

# CATEGORY 10: EXPANSION POTENTIAL

Maximum:

5 points

Ask:

Can the product eventually expand into:

Additional products?

Templates?

Premium versions?

Bundles?

Courses?

Memberships?

Services?

Automation?

Scoring:

0–1

Limited.

2–3

Moderate.

4–5

Strong.

---

# SCORE INTERPRETATION

## 85–100

Strong Opportunity

Good fit.

Clear problem.

Clear audience.

Good first product potential.

Still validate.

---

## 70–84

Promising Opportunity

Worth exploring.

May require refinement.

---

## 55–69

Potential Opportunity

Needs better:

Audience.

Problem.

Positioning.

Product format.

---

## Below 55

Weak First Opportunity

Not necessarily a bad market.

But may not currently be the best fit.

Explore alternatives.

---

# OPPORTUNITY TYPES

The AI should attempt to identify different types.

## STRONG FIT

Matches the user's profile.

## EASY START

Fast to create.

## HIGH EXPANSION

Could become a bigger business.

## UNDERRATED

Less obvious.

## UNEXPECTED

Something the user probably did not consider.

---

# RED FLAG CHECK

Reduce confidence if:

The product is too broad.

The audience is unclear.

The problem is unclear.

The user has no interest.

The product requires too much time.

The user needs expensive resources.

The product is difficult to explain.

The marketing path is unclear.

---

# MVP PRINCIPLE

Always ask:

"What is the smallest useful version?"

Example:

Instead of:

Complete Fitness Course.

Start with:

30-Day Beginner Gym Tracker.

Instead of:

Complete Parenting Program.

Start with:

New Parent Night Routine Checklist.

Instead of:

Social Media Course.

Start with:

30-Day Instagram Hook Library for Fitness Coaches.

Smaller products are easier to:

Create.

Launch.

Test.

Improve.

Sell.

---

# FINAL SCORING RULE

The AI must NOT automatically recommend the highest score.

Also consider:

User excitement.

Personal preference.

Available time.

Long-term goals.

The user must have final control.

The AI provides recommendations.

The user makes the decision.
