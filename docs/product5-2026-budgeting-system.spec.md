# 2026 Budgeting System
## Implementation-Ready Product Specification

**Product:** 2026 Budgeting System  
**Suggested launch price:** $19  
**Product type:** Interactive budgeting and financial organization system + downloadable spreadsheet/toolkit  
**Audience:** People who want a simple system for organizing income, expenses, savings, debt, goals, and recurring financial reviews.  
**Important positioning:** General budgeting and financial organization education. Not individualized investment, tax, legal, credit, or financial-planning advice.

---

# 1. Product Promise

### Core promise
Build a clear 2026 money system so you know what comes in, what goes out, what needs to be saved, what needs to be paid, and what to review next.

### Transformation

**Before**
- Money disappears without a clear picture
- Bills are scattered
- Irregular expenses cause surprises
- Savings are inconsistent
- Debt is difficult to track
- Financial goals stay vague

**After**
- Income is visible
- Expenses are organized
- Bills are planned
- Savings goals have targets
- Debt is tracked
- Sinking funds prepare for irregular expenses
- Monthly and weekly reviews keep the system current

### Core philosophy

> Visibility → Planning → Control → Review

The objective is not financial perfection. It is financial visibility and repeatable decision-making.

# 3. Module Structure

## MODULE 1: Your Financial Starting Point

### Outcome
Create a Financial Snapshot.

### Lessons

#### 1.1 Know Your Starting Point
Before building a budget, identify:
- Current income
- Regular expenses
- Debt
- Savings
- Major upcoming expenses
- Financial goals

#### 1.2 Net Position
Record assets and liabilities at a high level.

This is a snapshot, not a judgment.

#### 1.3 Financial Snapshot
Complete:
- Monthly income
- Monthly fixed expenses
- Typical variable spending
- Debt balances
- Savings
- Upcoming large expenses
- Current goals

**Output:** Financial Snapshot.

---

## MODULE 2: Understand Your Income

### Outcome
Create an Income System.

### Lessons

#### 2.1 Identify Every Income Source
Examples:
- Salary
- Freelance
- Business
- Side income
- Commission
- Benefits or other legitimate income

#### 2.2 Income Timing
Record:
- Source
- Expected amount
- Expected date
- Frequency
- Variability

#### 2.3 Build an Income Calendar
Map expected income across the year.

For variable income, use conservative planning assumptions rather than assuming the highest possible month will repeat.

**Output:** Income Tracker + Income Calendar.

---

## MODULE 3: Fixed Expenses

### Outcome
Create a Fixed Expense Tracker.

### Lessons

#### 3.1 What Is Fixed?
Examples:
- Rent
- Insurance
- Phone
- Internet
- Subscriptions
- Loan payments
- Regular transport commitments

#### 3.2 Review Recurring Commitments
For each expense:
- Amount
- Due date
- Frequency
- Payment method
- Necessary?
- Review date

#### 3.3 Annualize Recurring Expenses
Convert weekly/monthly/annual commitments into a comparable annual picture.

**Output:** Fixed Expense Tracker.

---

## MODULE 4: Variable Expenses

### Outcome
Understand spending patterns.

### Lessons

#### 4.1 Separate Fixed From Variable
Variable spending can change from week to week.

Examples:
- Food
- Entertainment
- Shopping
- Personal spending
- Household purchases
- Transport

#### 4.2 Track Before You Optimize
First observe actual spending.

Avoid creating unrealistic targets before you understand the baseline.

#### 4.3 Find Spending Patterns
Look for:
- Repeated purchases
- High-variance categories
- Unplanned spending
- Seasonal spikes
- Expenses that could be planned in advance

**Output:** Spending Tracker + Spending Analysis.

---

## MODULE 5: Debt

### Outcome
Create a Debt Dashboard.

### Lessons

#### 5.1 Build the Debt Inventory
Record:
- Creditor
- Balance
- Interest rate
- Minimum payment
- Due date
- Remaining term where known

#### 5.2 Understand the Cost
Interest and fees can affect how quickly balances decline.

Use actual account terms where available.

#### 5.3 Create a Repayment Plan
Compare available strategies and choose based on the person's circumstances.

Do not present one debt strategy as universally optimal.

**Output:** Debt Tracker + Debt Plan.

### Worksheet
For each debt:
- Current balance
- Minimum payment
- Interest rate
- Extra payment capacity
- Target date if chosen

---

## MODULE 6: Savings

### Outcome
Create a Savings System.

### Lessons

#### 6.1 Give Savings a Job
Savings can serve different purposes:
- Emergency reserve
- Short-term purchase
- Annual expense
- Long-term goal
- Planned large purchase

#### 6.2 Set Specific Targets
Define:
- Goal
- Target amount
- Current amount
- Remaining amount
- Target date
- Contribution frequency

#### 6.3 Automate Where Appropriate
If supported by the customer's bank/provider, recurring transfers can reduce the need to remember every contribution.

**Output:** Savings Tracker.

---

## MODULE 7: Emergency Fund

### Outcome
Create an Emergency Fund Plan.

### Lessons

#### 7.1 What an Emergency Fund Is
An emergency reserve is money set aside for unexpected or urgent expenses.

#### 7.2 Define Your Target
The appropriate target depends on circumstances such as income stability, essential expenses, dependents, insurance, and available support.

Avoid presenting a single number as universally correct.

#### 7.3 Build Gradually
Set:
- Current amount
- Target
- Contribution
- Review date

**Output:** Emergency Fund Tracker.

---

## MODULE 8: Goals & Sinking Funds

### Outcome
Plan irregular expenses before they become emergencies.

### Lessons

#### 8.1 What Is a Sinking Fund?
A sinking fund is money deliberately set aside for a known future expense.

Examples:
- Annual insurance
- Gifts
- Holidays
- Car maintenance
- School expenses
- Electronics
- Large purchases

#### 8.2 The Basic Formula

**Goal amount ÷ number of contribution periods = target contribution**

Example:

$600 annual expense ÷ 12 months = $50/month.

The example is illustrative, not financial advice.

#### 8.3 Build Your Sinking Fund Planner
Track:
- Goal
- Target
- Current balance
- Remaining
- Contribution
- Target date

**Output:** Sinking Fund Planner.

---

## MODULE 9: Build Your Monthly Budget

### Outcome
Create the 2026 Monthly Budget.

### Lessons

#### 9.1 Start With Income
Record expected income.

#### 9.2 Allocate the Money
Plan:
- Fixed expenses
- Variable expenses
- Debt
- Savings
- Goals/sinking funds

#### 9.3 Calculate Remaining Money

**Income − planned expenses − savings/debt allocations = remaining amount**

The goal is to understand the complete picture before spending.

#### 9.4 Give Every Category a Review Rule
For each category:
- Planned amount
- Actual amount
- Difference
- Reason for difference
- Adjustment if needed

**Output:** Monthly Budget.

---

## MODULE 10: Weekly Money Check-In

### Outcome
Create a repeatable weekly review.

### Lessons

#### 10.1 Why Weekly?
A short review can identify issues before the end of the month.

#### 10.2 Five-Minute Money Check
Ask:
1. What did I spend?
2. What bills are coming?
3. Did anything unexpected happen?
4. Am I still on track?
5. What needs adjusting?

#### 10.3 Make Adjustments
A budget is a plan, not a punishment.

Update future spending based on current information.

**Output:** Weekly Money Check-In.

---

## MODULE 11: Annual Financial Planning

### Outcome
Create the 2026 Annual Financial Planner.

### Lessons

#### 11.1 Look Beyond One Month
List:
- Annual bills
- Holidays
- Insurance
- Major purchases
- Education expenses
- Expected income changes
- Savings goals
- Debt goals

#### 11.2 Create the Annual Calendar
Place major expenses and income events across the year.

#### 11.3 Monthly Review of the Annual Plan
At each month-end:
- Update actuals
- Move completed goals
- Adjust upcoming targets
- Add new known expenses

**Output:** 2026 Annual Financial Planner.
